Why Sewage Is a Category 3 “Black Water” Emergency

Under the IICRC S500 standard, sewage is classified as Category 3 water —“black water” —the most contaminated category there is. It can carry bacteria, viruses, parasites, and other pathogens, along with chemicals and human waste. That classification isn’t a formality;it dictates how the job has to be done:

  • All porous materials that contacted the water must be removed and replaced. Contaminated drywall, carpet, pad, and insulation cannot be “dried and saved”—there is no safe way to disinfect them.
  • The work is treated as a biohazard. Technicians use personal protective equipment, containment, and hospital-grade disinfectants, not consumer products.

This is also why sewage cleanup costs more than a clean-water loss of the same size — the added safety and disposal requirements typically add 40–60% to the cost.

Common Causes of Sewage Backups

  • Main sewer line backups — a clog or collapse downstream, or a surcharge in the city main, pushes waste back up through the lowest drains in the building.
  • Toilet overflows with waste — a clog near the toilet that brings contaminated water onto the floor.
  • Flooding — heavy rain and flood conditions overwhelm municipal sewer systems and force sewage back into homes and basements.
  • Failed or clogged drain lines — because these come from the drainage side of the system, shutting off your water main will not stop them.

The Health Hazards — and Why You Shouldn’t DIY

Sewage exposure can cause serious gastrointestinal, respiratory, and skin illnesses, and the airborne contaminants alone make an unventilated backup dangerous to be near. Beyond the health risk, improper cleanup can leave hidden contamination behind and may even jeopardize an insurance claim. The safe and correct move is to stay out of the affected area and bring in certified professionals.

Our Sewage Cleanup Process

  1. Containment & safety setup. We isolate the affected area, set up PPE and containment, and protect the rest of the property from cross-contamination.
  2. Extraction & waste removal. Specialized pumps and extraction units remove sewage and standing water.
  3. Removal of contaminated materials. All affected porous materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Category 3 protocols.
  4. Cleaning & disinfection. Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated with professional-grade antimicrobials and disinfectants.
  5. HEPA air scrubbing & drying. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers clean the air while commercial dehumidifiers and air movers dry the structure to verified levels.
  6. Deodorization & rebuild-ready. Odors are neutralized and the area is verified clean, dry, and ready for reconstruction.

How Much Does Sewage Cleanup Cost?

Because of the biohazard handling, disposal, and material replacement involved, sewage cleanup typically runs $3,000–$15,000 depending on the size of the affected area, how far the contamination spread, and how much material has to be removed and rebuilt. The added safety requirements generally make it 40–60% more expensive than a clean-water loss of comparable size.

Does Insurance Cover Sewage Backups?

Often only if you’ve planned for it. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude sewer and drain backups as base coverage. Protection usually requires a separate water backup / sewer backup endorsement (rider) added to your policy. If the sewage came in as part of outside flooding, it falls under flood insurance rules instead. We document the loss thoroughly and work with your carrier to support whatever coverage applies.

Sewage and Mold

Like any water intrusion, an untreated sewage backup creates ideal conditions for mold within 24–48 hours —compounded by the organic contaminants sewage leaves behind. Prompt, professional removal and drying is the best way to avoid a follow-on mold remediation project.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is sewage backup dangerous?

Yes. Sewage is Category 3 black water containing pathogens and toxins. It poses real health risks on contact and through the air, so the area should be evacuated and handled by certified professionals.

Can I clean up a sewage backup myself?

It’s strongly discouraged. Without proper PPE, containment, and disinfection you risk illness, leaving hidden contamination behind, and potentially voiding your insurance claim.

What has to be thrown away after a sewage backup?

Porous materials that contacted the sewage — drywall, carpet and pad, insulation, and similar — must be removed and replaced. Non-porous surfaces can usually be cleaned and disinfected.

Will my homeowners insurance cover it?

Usually only if you carry a water/sewer backup endorsement, since standard policies typically exclude drain and sewer backups. Sewage from outside flooding falls under flood insurance rules.

Why won’t shutting off my water main stop it?

A sewage backup comes from the drainage side of the system, not your supply lines, so the water main has no effect on it.
